## Key Takeaways

- The abstract describes a move away from generic carbohydrate guidance toward guidance based on exercise duration, intensity, and carbohydrate type.
- It reports performance benefits from a mouth rinse or small amounts of carbohydrate during exercise lasting approximately 1 hour.
- It describes approximately 60 g/h for 2–3 hours with a single carbohydrate source and approximately 90 g/h for ultra-endurance events with multiple transportable carbohydrates.
- It states that recommendations may be lower when absolute exercise intensity and carbohydrate oxidation rates are low.

## Important Findings

- The abstract reports that a single carbohydrate source can be oxidized at rates up to approximately 60 g/h.
- For ultra-endurance events, the abstract reports a recommendation of approximately 90 g/h and states that multiple transportable carbohydrates are needed at such high ingestion rates to allow high oxidation rates and prevent carbohydrate accumulation in the intestine.
- The abstract states that carbohydrate intake advice is independent of body weight and training status, but dependent on the type and duration of activity.
