## Research Question / Purpose

The abstract states that the study investigated whether ingesting a high-rate mixture of glucose and fructose at 2.4 g/min would produce higher exogenous carbohydrate oxidation rates than previously observed and higher than glucose alone.

## Population / Context

Eight trained male cyclists with a reported VO2max of 68 ± 1 ml/kg per min cycled for 150 minutes at 50% of maximal power output (60 ± 1% VO2max) on three occasions. They consumed water, 1.2 g/min glucose, or 1.2 g/min glucose plus 1.2 g/min fructose.

## What Was Actually Examined

The study examined exogenous carbohydrate oxidation during prolonged cycling under water, glucose, and glucose-plus-fructose intake conditions, including peak oxidation rates and rates during the last 90 minutes of exercise.

## Key Takeaways

- The abstract reports that glucose plus fructose produced higher exogenous carbohydrate oxidation than glucose alone.
- The abstract reports approximately 50% higher oxidation during the last 90 minutes with glucose plus fructose than with glucose alone.
- The abstract reports a peak exogenous carbohydrate oxidation rate of approximately 1.75 g/min under the 2.4 g/min combined glucose-plus-fructose intake condition.

## Important Findings

- Peak exogenous carbohydrate oxidation was 1.75 (SE 0.11) g/min with glucose plus fructose and 1.06 (SE 0.05) g/min with glucose alone; the abstract reports P<0.01.
- During the last 90 minutes, exogenous carbohydrate oxidation was 1.49 (SE 0.08) g/min with glucose plus fructose and 0.99 (SE 0.06) g/min with glucose alone; the abstract reports P<0.05.
- The abstract describes the exercise protocol as 150 minutes of cycling at 50% of maximal power output, corresponding to 60 ± 1% VO2max.
