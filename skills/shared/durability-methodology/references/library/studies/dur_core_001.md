## Research Question / Purpose

The review asks why routine physiological profiling should account for changes in physiological characteristics during prolonged exercise and proposes directions for quantifying individual athlete durability.

## What Was Actually Examined

The abstract examines the changing nature of physiological-profiling attributes during prolonged exercise, the consequences of ignoring those changes, and the need for profiling models that quantify the onset and magnitude of deterioration over time.

## Key Takeaways

- The review abstract reports that physiological attributes measured when athletes are well rested can change during prolonged exercise.
- The abstract argues that these changes have implications for the interpretation and use of physiological profiling.
- The abstract proposes quantifying durability as the time of onset and magnitude of deterioration in physiological-profiling characteristics.

## Important Findings

- The abstract reports that literature and field data support changes in physiological-profiling attributes during prolonged exercise.
- The abstract states that routine profiling models do not account for these shifts.
- The abstract links unaccounted shifts with potential inaccuracies in intensity regulation, training-adaptation quantification, training-load programming and monitoring, and exercise-performance prediction.
