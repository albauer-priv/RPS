## Research Question / Purpose

The review examined the effect of fructose:glucose or fructose:maltodextrin composites versus glucose or maltodextrin alone on endurance performance and considered mechanistic associations involving carbohydrate metabolism, gastrointestinal responses, fluid absorption, and sensory responses.

## Population / Context

Fourteen studies containing estimates of 2.5–3.0-hour endurance performance, mostly in men and mostly in cycling. The abstract covers composite carbohydrate ratios of 0.5:1–1.0:1 and ingestion rates of 1.3–2.4 g carbohydrate·min⁻¹.

## What Was Actually Examined

The abstract examined endurance-performance comparisons between fructose–glucose or fructose–maltodextrin composite beverages and isocaloric glucose or maltodextrin beverages. It also summarized associations with exogenous carbohydrate oxidation, fluid absorption, gastrointestinal distress, and the effects of different fructose:glucose ratios and carbohydrate ingestion rates.

## Key Takeaways

- The review abstract reports small to moderate endurance-power benefits from composite carbohydrate beverages compared with single-saccharide formulations in the studied context.
- The abstract reports that higher ingestion rates within the examined range were associated with larger effects for 0.5:1 composites.
- The abstract identifies 0.7:1–1.0:1 solutions as being absorbed fastest and reports a 0.8:1 ratio as associated with the highest exogenous carbohydrate energy and endurance power at 1.5–1.8 g·min⁻¹.
- The abstract concludes that 0.5:1–1.0:1 composite beverages at 1.3–2.4 g·min⁻¹ likely benefit 2.5–3.0-hour endurance power versus isocaloric single-saccharide beverages.

## Important Findings

- The abstract reports 1–9% mean-power enhancements, with a 95% confidence interval of 0–19%, for 0.5:1–1.0:1 composites ingested at 1.3–2.4 g carbohydrate·min⁻¹ versus isocaloric glucose or maltodextrin.
- For 0.5:1 composites, the abstract reports 4–9% improvement at at least 1.7 g·min⁻¹ versus 1–3% at 1.4–1.6 g·min⁻¹.
- The higher-ingestion-rate effects were associated with increased exogenous carbohydrate oxidation, unilateral fluid absorption, and lower gastrointestinal distress relative to control.
- The abstract reports fastest absorption for solutions containing a 0.7:1–1.0:1 fructose:glucose ratio and the highest exogenous carbohydrate energy and endurance power for a 0.8:1 ratio at 1.5–1.8 g·min⁻¹.
