## Key Takeaways

- The review abstract describes short-term insufficient training stimulus as potentially affecting endurance-related physiological and performance adaptations.

## Important Findings

- The review abstract reports that, in highly trained athletes, short-term detraining is characterized by a rapid decline in maximal oxygen uptake and blood volume.
- The abstract reports reduced stroke volume, reduced maximal cardiac output, impaired ventilatory efficiency, and impaired endurance performance in highly trained athletes.
- The abstract reports that these cardiorespiratory changes are more moderate in recently trained individuals.
- The abstract reports increased reliance on carbohydrate metabolism during exercise, alongside lowered lipase activity, GLUT-4 content, glycogen level, and lactate threshold.
- The abstract reports reductions in capillary density and oxidative enzyme activities at the muscle level.
- The abstract reports that training-induced changes in muscle fibre cross-sectional area are reversed, while strength-performance declines are limited.
- The abstract reports hormonal changes including reduced insulin sensitivity and reversal of short-term training-induced adaptations in fluid-electrolyte-regulating hormones.
