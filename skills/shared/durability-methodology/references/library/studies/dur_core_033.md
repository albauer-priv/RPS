## Research Question / Purpose

The abstract states that the study compared high carbohydrate intake of 120 g/h with 90 g/h and 60 g/h during a mountain marathon to examine effects on exercise load and exercise-induced muscle-damage markers.

## Population / Context

Twenty male elite runners with prior nutritional and gut training completed a mountain marathon with approximately 4000 m of cumulative slope. The groups consumed 120 g/h, 90 g/h, or 60 g/h of carbohydrate during the event.

## What Was Actually Examined

The study examined internal exercise load based on rate of perceived exertion during and after the marathon, together with creatine kinase, lactate dehydrogenase, glutamic oxaloacetic transaminase, urea, and creatinine measured before the race and 24 hours afterward.

## Key Takeaways

- The abstract reports lower internal exercise load with 120 g/h than with 90 g/h or 60 g/h.
- The abstract reports lower 24-hour creatine kinase, lactate dehydrogenase, and glutamic oxaloacetic transaminase values with 120 g/h than with 90 g/h or 60 g/h.
- The abstract reports a close correlation between exercise-induced muscle damage and internal exercise load.
- The findings are bounded to previously gut-trained male elite runners in a mountain-marathon event.

## Important Findings

- Internal exercise load was 3805 ± 281 AU in the 120 g/h group, compared with 4688 ± 705 AU in the 60 g/h group and 4692 ± 716 AU in the 90 g/h group; the abstract reports p = 0.019 and η2p = 0.471.
- The 120 g/h group had significantly lower creatine kinase 24 hours after the race than the 60 g/h and 90 g/h groups; p = 0.019; η2p = 0.373.
- The 120 g/h group had significantly lower lactate dehydrogenase 24 hours after the race than the 60 g/h and 90 g/h groups; p < 0.001; η2p = 0.615.
- The 120 g/h group had significantly lower glutamic oxaloacetic transaminase 24 hours after the race than the 60 g/h and 90 g/h groups; p = 0.003; η2p = 0.500.
- Exercise-induced muscle damage and exercise load were correlated at R = 0.742; p < 0.001.
- No result for urea or creatinine is reported in the supplied abstract.
