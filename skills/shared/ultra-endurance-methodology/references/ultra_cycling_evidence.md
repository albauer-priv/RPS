# Ultra-Endurance Cycling Evidence

Evidence backing for the ultra-endurance methodology defined in `SKILL.md`.

Notation: `ult_core_*` = ultra-specific peer-reviewed sources; `ult_applied_*` = practitioner
sources. Sources already in the durability evidence library are cross-referenced by their
`dur_core_*` id.

## Core scientific sources

| id | authors | year | title | outlet | locator | key finding |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ult_core_001` | Spragg J, Leo P, Swart J | 2023 | The relationship between training characteristics and durability in professional cyclists across a competitive season | Eur J Sport Sci 23(4):489–498 | PMID 35239466 — DOI 10.1080/17461391.2022.2049886 | Training time below VT1 correlates with improvement in fatigued power profile (durability); shift toward polarized intensity distribution associates with improvement in fatigued 12-min power; fresh power profile is more stable than the fatigued profile across a season |
| `ult_core_002` | Mateo-March M, Leo P, Muriel X, Javaloyes A, Mujika I, Barranco-Gil D, Pallarés JG, Lucia A, Valenzuela PL | 2024 | Is all work the same? | `ult_core_003` | Leo P, Mateo-March M, Giorgi A, Muriel X, Javaloyes A, Barranco-Gil D, Pallarés JG, Lucia A, Mujika I, Valenzuela PL | 2024 | The influence of high-intensity work on the record power profile of under-23, Pro Team, and World Tour cyclists | Int J Sports Physiol Perform 19(6):545–549 | PMID 38531349 — DOI 10.1123/ijspp.2023-0451 | World Tour cyclists tolerate greater levels of high-intensity accumulated work before power-profile decline than under-23 or Pro Team cyclists; durability under high-intensity preload improves with training level — durable profile is trainable |
| `ult_core_004` | Voet JG, van Erp T, Viribay A, de Koning JJ, Lamberts RP | 2025 | Training characteristics related to (the changes in) durability in semiprofessional cyclists | Int J Sports Physiol Perform 20(5):644–652 | PMID 40054482 — DOI 10.1123/ijspp.2024-0321 | No single training load metric predicts durability changes; CarbOx shows strong correlation with training volume (fresh state) and with time below LT1 (fatigued state); same training affects fresh and fatigued performance differently — implications for training plan design |
| `dur_core_004` | Spragg J, Leo P, Swart J | 2023 | The relationship between physiological characteristics and durability in male professional cyclists | Med Sci Sports Exerc 55(1):133–140 | PMID 35977108 — DOI 10.1249/MSS.0000000000003024 | Durability (ΔCP) correlates positively with relative VO2max, gross efficiency, VT1 power; negatively with CarbOx at 200W — lower carbohydrate oxidation at submaximal intensity predicts better durability; confirmed in library as `dur_core_004` |

**"Is all work the same?" is the single most critical ultra-endurance planning rule**: work above CP
impairs subsequent performance far more than equal kJ below CP. This justifies the kJ-first /
volume-first approach throughout all phases.

## Transfer boundaries

- `ult_core_001`, `ult_core_002`, `ult_core_003`: derived from professional and semi-professional
  competitive road cyclists; the durability principles transfer to ultra/brevet context but the
  specific kJ thresholds (2000 kJ as "pre-fatigue" marker) should be scaled to event duration —
  ultra events expose 4,000–20,000+ kJ accumulated; the fatigue-under-work principle applies at all scales
- `dur_core_004`: professional male cyclists only; physiological determinants of durability are
  expected to transfer across athlete levels with individual variation
- `ult_applied_001` (Kinzlbauer): practitioner source — not peer-reviewed; represents best-practice
  coaching synthesis from ultra cycling (RAAM preparation, PBP-level events)
- All sources are based on cycling; for run/multisport ultra events, durability mechanisms are
  comparable but timing and volume targets differ

## What this evidence justifies

**Justifies:**
- Z1/Z2 dominance (time below VT1) as the primary durability-building stimulus
- "Not all kJ equal" — work above CP carries a higher durability tax than work below CP
- Prioritizing fat oxidation development through high-volume low-intensity training
- Longer Base phases (≥ 8–12 weeks) for metabolic adaptation
- B2B long rides and preload-then-effort structure as direct durability training
- Durable profile is trainable — not fixed at initial fitness level

**Does not justify:**
- Eliminating all Z3/Z4 work — bounded high-intensity has a role for VO2max ceiling and neuromuscular maintenance
- Specific kJ targets per training day or per week — those are in `load-governance`
- TSS/CTL/ATL as primary planning currency — these metrics do not capture durability
- Replacing periodization structure from `periodization-methodology`
