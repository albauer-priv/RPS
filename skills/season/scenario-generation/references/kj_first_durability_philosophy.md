# kJ-First Durability Philosophy for Scenario Generation

Sources: `skills/shared/durability-methodology/SKILL.md`, `skills/season/load-governance/references/progression_guardrails.md`

This reference summarizes the foundational planning principles the scenario generation layer applies
when differentiating scenarios and choosing cadence, domain permissions, and load posture.

---

## Core definition

**Durability** = preservation of usable submaximal performance over long durations under accumulated
work — not fresh peak ability. Ultra/brevet events expose durability, not peak power.

**Repeatability** = ability to reproduce the next load step or the next week without hidden recovery
debt. A training week that cannot be repeated safely is not a useful training week.

---

## kJ-first principle

Weekly kJ (mechanical work) is the primary scenario identity dimension. Scenarios must differ in
their kJ-envelope, load posture, and recovery rhythm — not just in which intensity domains appear.

When time budget makes clear kJ separation unrealistic, scenarios must differ through risk contract,
density, specificity, and recovery tolerance rather than artificial kJ inflation.

---

## Progression order

Progress load through these axes strictly in order — do not skip ahead:

1. `kJ / time` — more volume (longer sessions, more total work)
2. `frequency` — more training days per week
3. `density / complexity` — more loading weeks per phase, higher block stress
4. `intensity` — harder domains (TEMPO → SWEET_SPOT → THRESHOLD → VO2MAX)

Intensity is the **last** overload lever. Apply it only after stable tolerance to the current kJ
level is demonstrated. Do not simultaneously push kJ ramp and increase intensity density.

---

## Progressive overload guardrails

Week-over-week kJ ramp:
- Conservative: +5% to +8% (high life stress, masters, poor recovery)
- Standard: +8% to +12% (normal recovery and execution stability)
- Aggressive (rare): +12% to +18% (highly robust, consistently strong recovery)
- Hard cap: avoid sustained ramps above +15% outside explicit special cases

Deload target:
- `DL_kJ = BL_kJ × 0.60–0.80` (baseline anchored, preferred)
- Deload must reduce kJ materially — not only reduce intensity

---

## Decision hierarchy

When choosing between scenario options:

1. Preserve sustainable weekly and seasonal repeatability.
2. Protect recovery structure before cosmetic symmetry or catch-up behavior.
3. Increase load primarily through durable volume/work expansion, not intensity stacking.
4. Prefer conservative continuity over heroic compensation after missed sessions.
5. When in doubt, choose the option the athlete can likely repeat next week.

---

## Scenario implication

These principles define what "Scenario A", "Scenario B", and "Scenario C" mean:

- **Scenario A** = maximum repeatability, minimal intensity, robustness to disruption
- **Scenario B** = realistic durable kJ-envelope, systematic progression, balanced recovery
- **Scenario C** = upper plausible load, higher density and specificity-under-fatigue, still
  grounded in durability (not defined by intensity escalation)

Scenario ambition comes from **specificity and fatigue exposure**, not from domain escalation.
Intensity is a shaping lever within a chosen scenario, not the primary differentiation axis.

---

## Long-session dominance

Ultra/brevet events are long-ride events. Long-session share should be managed:
- Flag "long-ride dominated" when a single session > 50% of weekly time
- Repeated dominance favors `2:1` or `2:1:1` cadence to preserve recovery structure
- The kJ of the long session must be balanced against weekday session quality

---

## What the scenario layer must make explicit

Before handing off to season planning, the scenario layer must state:

- **Progression driver**: what drives load growth in this scenario — duration-led, frequency-led,
  or quality/intensity-led?
- **Recovery rhythm**: how often does the athlete fully reset, and what does that reset feel like?
- **Fatigue exposure**: how much accumulated stress builds before the reset?
- **Risk posture**: what disruption can this scenario absorb before it collapses?

Season planning executes these as numbers (kJ corridors, phase load bands, ramp rates). The scenario
layer's job is to make the character choice concrete and unambiguous so season planning has no
guesswork to do.
